# Terrane Art Direction

**Right now, the blocks feel somewhat like a colorful architectural model or a tabletop
strategy game. I would push them toward the appearance of a precision-engineered,
architectural data visualization.**

Not photorealistic. Not flashy. Just exceptionally deliberate, refined, and consistent.

## What I notice and what I would improve

### 1. Refine the geometry and edge language

Priority: high.

The blocks currently have fairly pronounced bevels, rounded-looking transitions, and bright
edge highlights. Some of the larger blocks, particularly `DATA`, `SITE`, and `.GIT`, look a
little soft or inflated.

I'd recommend:

- **Sharper, more disciplined silhouettes.** Think finely machined architectural components
  rather than molded plastic.
- **Much subtler bevels.** Retain enough edge definition to communicate three-dimensionality
  without creating thick, glossy borders.
- **Consistent edge treatments.** A small file block and a large directory block should look
  like members of the same design family.
- **Cleaner vertical corners.** The corners should feel precise and structural rather than
  slightly cushioned.

The goal is not to eliminate dimensionality. It's to make every edge appear intentional.

### 2. Give the blocks a more sophisticated material identity

This might be the single biggest aesthetic opportunity.

At present, several blocks have the appearance of colored plastic, particularly the violet
video rectangles and pink image blocks. The shiny highlights and contrasting side faces
contribute to this.

I would establish one unified material language across the visualization:

**Satin-finished, precision-cut architectural solids.**

That means visually:

- Smooth, mostly matte top surfaces.
- Soft, controlled highlights rather than glossy rims.
- Solid, substantial-looking vertical faces.
- Consistent material character across all categories.
- No unnecessary surface ornamentation.

The material should feel calm, expensive, and engineered.

Think of a premium architectural scale model or a physical scientific instrument.

### 3. Reconsider how color behaves across the cube faces

Priority: high.

I actually like the fundamental idea of coloring blocks by file category. It makes the
terrain immediately useful.

However, the current combination of purple, pink, green, blue, and teal creates a fairly
playful visual impression, particularly where multiple colors are visible on the sides of a
block.

I'd retain the categorical associations but refine their character.

| Category  | Suggested visual character  |
| --------- | --------------------------- |
| Images    | Muted mineral coral         |
| Video     | Deep architectural plum     |
| Documents | Desaturated steel blue      |
| Data      | Earthy sage / mineral olive |
| Code      | Restrained oxidized teal    |
| Other     | Warm graphite / stone gray  |
| Archives  | Muted bronze                |

These aren't necessarily replacements for your existing palette. They're the direction I'd
take its saturation and sophistication.

More importantly, **the top and sides of each block should feel like one coherent object**.

For example, a purple video block might have a muted plum top, a deeper plum front, and a
slightly darker side.

I would particularly investigate the bright multicolored horizontal bands visible around
some of the taller blocks. If those bands communicate composition or nested content,
preserve the information, but consider expressing it through a thin, carefully integrated
accent band rather than several visually competing layers.

### 4. Establish a more disciplined height and proportion system

One of the things I notice is how dramatically the blocks differ in perceived thickness.

The purple video blocks are almost flat pavers. The image blocks have more substantial
bodies. The taller `DATA` and `.GIT` masses read almost like buildings.

This can absolutely work, but it needs a coherent visual rationale.

My recommendation would be to establish three recognizable physical forms:

- **Containment platforms.** Thin, understated architectural foundations. These communicate
  hierarchy rather than data mass.
- **Directory masses.** Substantial, confidently proportioned blocks with a consistent sense
  of volume.
- **Individual file tiles.** Lower-profile, crisp, solid pieces that feel part of the same
  object family.

Conceptual geometry language only—not proposed literal dimensions or a replacement for any
existing data encoding.

If height currently represents a metric, I would absolutely preserve that relationship. The
improvement is in making the proportions and surface treatment feel systematic, not
artificially equalizing all the blocks.

### 5. Give the blocks more breathing room

Priority: high.

Look at the cluster of purple video blocks in the upper-left portion of BEASTIQUE.

Individually, the shapes are understandable. Collectively, their spacing and deep dividing
seams make them look almost like irregular pavement.

The same happens with the dense multicolored blocks on the right side.

I would use a deliberate spacing hierarchy:

- Narrow, consistent channels between individual file blocks.
- Wider, more generous separation between distinct groups.
- A clearly defined margin where blocks meet their parent platform.

This would give the overall arrangement a more architectural rhythm.

The negative space should be as carefully designed as the solid geometry.

### 6. Reduce the visual weight of the stacked foundations

The cream-colored platforms beneath `PROJECTS`, `BEASTIQUE`, `STUDIO`, and the nested groups
are doing useful work.

They make the filesystem hierarchy physically understandable.

However, they currently have thick rims, pronounced light edges, and fairly deep shadowed
separations. The cumulative effect is a series of stacked presentation trays.

I would make the foundations:

- Thinner and more architectural.
- Less glossy around the edges.
- More consistent in thickness at each hierarchy level.
- Slightly more neutral than the colored content blocks.

In other words, the platforms should establish structure without competing with the objects
sitting on them.

A good foundation is visually quiet. The data is the focal point.

### 7. Improve the typography as part of the cube design

The top-facing labels are essential to these objects, so their treatment matters.

Right now, `DATA`, `SITE`, `.GIT`, `FRAMES`, and `ZOONIVERSE` have noticeably different
visual weight depending on the size and viewing angle of the block.

I'd aim for a strict labeling vocabulary.

Large directory blocks should have confident, consistently positioned names with controlled
tracking. Smaller blocks should use quieter labels, and the tiniest objects shouldn't be
forced to carry text that cannot be read comfortably.

I'd also pay particular attention to contrast. Some labels are considerably easier to read
than others.

The ideal is for labels to appear **integral to the surface**, rather than visually stamped
onto a block as an afterthought.

### 8. Make indicators and selection states feel purpose-built

There are several black, downward-pointing markers hovering above the purple blocks.

Whatever their functional meaning, their current silhouette is quite prominent and feels
visually separate from the rest of the geometry.

I would explore a more restrained appearance: a small geometric indicator, a precise colored
edge accent, or a subtle surface-level distinction.

Similarly, an active or selected block should feel deliberately identified, not simply
brighter, taller, or more colorful.

Think **instrument-grade precision**, rather than game-style highlighting.

### 9. Improve how the cubes read as a collection at different scales

The close-up BEASTIQUE view makes individual objects much easier to distinguish. In the
wider PROJECTS view, many smaller blocks merge into a busy patchwork.

That's an important aesthetic issue for a filesystem visualization containing thousands of
objects.

I would want the visual hierarchy to remain compelling at both scales:

- From farther away, the composition should read as distinct, organized masses with
  recognizable category colors.
- Up close, individual objects should reveal carefully designed proportions, surfaces,
  edges, and labels.

The overall form should never depend on seeing every tiny subdivision to look good.

## The art direction I'd establish for TERRANE

### Precision Architectural Terrain

A proposed visual identity for the 3D object system.

- Mineral satin surfaces
- Controlled category color
- Quiet structural bases

---

- **Geometry:** Crisp, precision-cut rectilinear solids.
- **Material:** Matte to softly satin, never excessively glossy.
- **Edges:** Extremely controlled; subtle highlights and clean silhouettes.
- **Color:** Semantic, harmonized, and slightly desaturated.
- **Depth:** Purposeful, consistent, and meaningful.
- **Spacing:** Organized at both individual-object and group levels.
- **Typography:** Integrated, restrained, and hierarchical.
- **Overall personality:** Scientific instrument meets contemporary architectural
  visualization.
